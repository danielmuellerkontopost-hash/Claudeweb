# B1 Constructive PRO – Redetext

Today we are debating the resolution: "The United States federal government should enact a moratorium on hyperscale data center construction." I am Daniel Müller, and we stand in affirmation.

Two definitions. According to the market research firm IDC, a hyperscale data center has at least 5,000 servers on at least 10,000 square feet. *[S2]* And according to Cornell Law School, a moratorium suspends an activity "for a period of time" – or "until the purpose for which the moratorium was granted is satisfied." *[S1]* Our purpose is simple: construction resumes as soon as binding federal rules are in place.

**Contention one: Hyperscale data centers are making American lives worse.**

Meet Beverly and Jeff Morris from rural Georgia. In 2018, Meta started building a data center campus next door. A few months later, their dishwasher, ice maker, washing machine and toilet stopped working. The water came out full of sediment. Beverly told the New York Times: "I'm scared to drink our own water." *[S18]* Meta denies any connection – but Georgia families have now taken Meta to court. *[S19]*

Now go to Boxtown, a historically Black neighborhood in Memphis. Elon Musk's xAI built its Colossus supercomputer there – designed for 150 megawatts, enough for 100,000 homes *[S21]* – and powered it with 35 gas turbines without Clean Air Act permits. *[S20]* Shelby County already has the highest rate of children hospitalized for asthma in Tennessee. *[S22]* At a public hearing, resident Alexis Humphreys held up her inhaler and asked, in tears: "How come I can't breathe at home and y'all get to breathe at home?" *[S20]*

And you don't have to live next door to pay. On PJM, the grid serving 67 million Americans *[S14]*, the wholesale cost of power rose 76 percent in the first quarter of 2026 – driven by data centers. The independent market monitor warns: "The price impacts on customers have been very large and are not reversible." *[S23]*

These are not isolated cases. This spring, Gallup found that 71 percent of Americans oppose an AI data center in their area – more than oppose a nuclear plant. *[S24]* Over 800 local opposition groups have formed in 49 states. *[S25]*

**Contention two: Rules first, then concrete.**

Why pause instead of regulating alongside construction? Because concrete cannot be un-poured. Once a campus stands, its turbines, power lines and water use are locked in for decades. And today, the rules are not ready. Three examples.

First, household protection is a promise, not a law. In March, seven tech giants signed the White House "Ratepayer Protection Pledge" – voluntary and non-binding. *[S26]* Only 25 states have approved even one special tariff for large loads *[S27]*, and the federal regulator FERC still has no national rule for connecting them to the grid. *[S28]*

Second, the rules arrive after the damage. xAI's turbines ran for well over a year before the EPA clarified in January 2026 that turbines like these need permits. *[S29]* Boxtown breathed the exhaust in the meantime.

Third, people are kept in the dark. NPR reported this August how non-disclosure agreements, under code names like "Project Lightning", hid a Louisiana data center deal from residents until it was approved. *[S30]* And no federal law requires data centers to report how much water and electricity they consume. *[S31]*

A moratorium is exactly the right tool. The U.S. Supreme Court wrote in 2002 that moratoria "are used widely among land-use planners to preserve the status quo while formulating a more permanent development strategy." *[S32]* Singapore paused in 2019 and reopened in 2022 with strict efficiency rules. *[S33]* Indianapolis' council voted this August to pause until the end of 2027 to write its rules. *[S34]* But more than 300 local moratoria and bans are a patchwork *[S35]* – while one grid like PJM crosses 13 states. Only Washington can set one floor.

So our moratorium lasts until three things are law: households stop paying for data centers, communities are told the truth before the bulldozers arrive, and permits come before turbines. Then we build – but we build right.

Beverly Morris deserves to drink her water. Alexis Humphreys deserves to breathe at home. That is why we proudly affirm.

# Frontlines – erwartete Contra-Angriffe auf B1

**F1 „Anecdotes, not evidence.“**
→ The stories show the face; the data shows the scale: Gallup, 71 % oppose *[S24]*; 833 opposition groups in 49 states *[S25]*; 76 % wholesale price increase on PJM, measured by the independent market monitor *[S23]*.

**F2 „Meta denies it – no proven causation in Georgia.“**
→ We said that ourselves. The point is: no rule required a groundwater study *before* blasting began. A court now has to sort it out *after* the damage. That is exactly Contention 2.

**F3 „xAI is one bad actor, and the EPA already fixed it.“**
→ Our point exactly: the fix came about 18 months *after* the turbines started *[S29]*. Rules after concrete. And xAI is no outsider: it is one of the seven signatories of the Ratepayer Protection Pledge *[S26]*.

**F4 „Targeted rules already work: large-load tariffs, the pledge“ (deine Contra-Linie C6).**
→ The pledge is voluntary *[S26]*. 25 states have a large-load tariff, which means 25 don't *[S27]*. Tariffs address costs, not water, air or secrecy. FERC has not finished its rule *[S28]*. A moratorium gives these rules a deadline instead of an open end.

**F5 „Companies will just build abroad“ (deine Contra-Linie C1).**
→ (1) Our moratorium is conditional, not indefinite: the faster the rules come, the sooner building resumes. (2) Singapore paused, wrote rules and reopened with stricter standards *[S33]*. (3) The bigger threat to the buildout is already here: local opposition blocked or delayed about $130 billion of projects in one quarter *[S25]*. Clear rules rebuild trust. Without them, people stop projects one county at a time.

**F6 „Local governments can handle it.“**
→ They are trying: there are more than 300 local moratoria and bans *[S35]*. But one grid crosses 13 states, so costs spill over. Maryland farmland is being crossed by a 500-kV line for Virginia's data centers (SB4). A patchwork with loopholes can't set one national floor.

**F7 „Until the purpose is satisfied = forever.“**
→ The purpose is concrete and checkable: three laws (cost allocation, transparency, permits before turbines). Congress already has bills on the table, e.g. the Data Center Water and Energy Transparency Act *[S31]*.

**F8 „AI race / China.“**
→ A short, conditional pause doesn't decide the AI race. Public trust does: 71 % already say no *[S24]*. Building fast without rules produces exactly the backlash that is stopping projects today *[S25]*.

**F9 „PJM prices: all data centers, not only hyperscale.“**
→ The load growth the market monitor describes comes from large data center load, meaning the big campuses. 5,000 servers is a low threshold, so practically every new large campus is hyperscale under IDC *[S2]*.
